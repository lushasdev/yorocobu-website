This is not an entry about Yorocobu. It is the list of renderings you must use
verbatim when answering in Japanese, so that the same thing is not called three
names across three answers.

The knowledge base stays in English as the single source of truth about the
facts. Translate the rest of it at answer time; do not improvise anything listed
below.

## Never translated

These stay exactly as written, in Latin script, in Japanese answers:

| Term | Rendering | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Yorocobu | `Yorocobu` | ヨロコブ, ヨロコブ社, 喜ぶ as the company name |
| Yorocobu LLC | `Yorocobu LLC` | 有限会社ヨロコブ, 合同会社 anything |
| Joy | `Joy` | 喜び, ジョイ |
| Co-Founder | `Co-Founder` | 共同創業者 as a *title* — see below |
| React, React Native, Swift, SwiftUI, Flutter | as written | カタカナ transliterations |
| Morehead-Cain | `Morehead-Cain` | モアヘッド・ケイン |
| UNC Chapel Hill | `UNC Chapel Hill` | ノースカロライナ大学 |
| Keio University | `Keio University` or `慶應義塾大学` | any other form |
| Wyoming, Chapel Hill | as written | ワイオミング, チャペルヒル |

「Co-Founder」 is the published job title and stays in Latin when it is being
used as one. Describing the arrangement in prose — 「二人で創業しました」 — is
fine; 「共同創業者という肩書き」 is not, because that is not the title.

If Ethan's name is ever written in katakana — in a heading, a caption, anywhere

no authoritative Japanese spelling for them either.

## Japanese terms the site owns

These are Japanese already and are quoted as-is, in answers in either language:

| Term | Meaning as the site gives it |
|---|---|
| 喜ぶ | the verb the company name comes from, *to be glad* |
| 喜 | the kanji used as the company mark |
| 忘れ者 | the documentary's title, *forgotten person* |
| 忘れ物 | the everyday word the title plays on, a lost or left-behind object |

Never gloss 喜ぶ or 忘れ者 beyond the readings above. Never write the company
name as 喜ぶ in running text: the etymology is a fact about the name, not a way
of spelling it.

## The company, in one sentence

When a Japanese answer needs to describe what Yorocobu does, build it from this
rather than re-inventing the phrasing each time:

> Yorocobu LLC が手がけているのは二つです。目的に合わせた AI エージェント開発と、
> オーダーメイドのソフトウェア開発です。

「目的に合わせた」 carries *tailored* without claiming a market: it says the
work is fitted to the job, which is what the English says, and nothing about who
the job belongs to. 「オーダーメイド」 is the ordinary Japanese word for work
made to order, and reads as plainly in business copy as *custom* does in
English, where 「カスタム」 would be a transliteration standing in for a real word.

The two lines are the whole of it. Never add a third, and never name a market,
a sector, or a customer type in either of them.
